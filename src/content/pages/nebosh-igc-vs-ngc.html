The Core Difference Between NEBOSH IGC and NGC: Legislative Framework

The NEBOSH International General Certificate (IGC) and the NEBOSH National General Certificate (NGC) are structurally identical qualifications — same assessment format, same grading thresholds, same command words, same number of units — with one critical difference: the legislative framework each qualification requires candidates to apply in their assignments.

The IGC references international frameworks: ILO-OSH 2001 (the International Labour Organization's Guidelines on Occupational Safety and Health Management Systems) and ISO 45001:2018 (the international H&S management system standard that replaced OHSAS 18001). Candidates answering an IG1 OBE task about managing workplace hazards should reference ISO 45001 risk controls and ILO-OSH 2001 management principles. Referencing HSWA 1974 or MHSWR 1999 — UK domestic statutes — in an IGC answer demonstrates a misunderstanding of the qualification's framework and will not attract legislative-reference marks from the examiner.

The NGC references UK domestic legislation. The core statutes and regulations that NGC candidates must know and apply include: the Health and Safety at Work etc. Act 1974 (HSWA) — the primary UK H&S statute establishing employer duties under Section 2 (employees) and Section 3 (non-employees); the Management of Health and Safety at Work Regulations 1999 (MHSWR) — requiring risk assessment under Regulation 3 and H&S arrangements under Regulation 5; the Control of Substances Hazardous to Health Regulations 2002 (COSHH); the Reporting of Injuries, Diseases and Dangerous Occurrences Regulations 2013 (RIDDOR); the Manual Handling Operations Regulations 1992; the Personal Protective Equipment at Work Regulations 1992; the Provision and Use of Work Equipment Regulations 1998 (PUWER); and the Workplace (Health, Safety and Welfare) Regulations 1992.

This difference in legislative content shapes the entire NG1 and IG1 OBE answer strategy. A candidate who learns the NGC's UK legislative framework and then sits the IGC OBE will score significantly below their knowledge level by referencing the wrong frameworks. The reverse is equally true.

NEBOSH IGC: Unit Structure, Assessment, and What Assignments Require

The NEBOSH IGC consists of two units. IG1 — Management of International Health and Safety — is assessed by an Open Book Exam. IG2 — International Workplace Risk Assessment — is assessed by a practical report completed in the candidate's own workplace.

The IG1 OBE runs over a 48-hour window. Before the window opens, candidates receive a workplace scenario document — a detailed description of a workplace, its hazards, management practices, and safety culture. During the 48-hour window, candidates complete 15 tasks all anchored to that scenario and submit via the NEBOSH online portal. Every answer must reference the specific scenario. An accurate answer about ISO 45001 management principles that makes no connection to the described workplace does not satisfy IG1 marking criteria.

Six command words appear across the 15 tasks: Suggest, Describe, Explain, Outline, Justify, and Identify. Each requires a different response depth. Suggest asks for a practical proposal — no single correct answer, but it must be scenario-grounded. Describe requires a full detailed account. Explain requires causal reasoning. Outline requires the key points. Justify requires reasons linked to scenario evidence. Identify requires a brief named list.

IG1 grading: Distinction at 80%+; Credit at 65–79%; Pass at 45–64%; Refer below 45%. IG2 is graded separately on practical report quality — hazard identification breadth (minimum 5 hazards), risk matrix accuracy (Likelihood 1–5 × Consequence 1–5), control measure hierarchy (elimination through PPE), and priority action quality.

The international legislative framework applies throughout. IG1 answers referencing HSG65 (a UK HSE document), HSWA 1974, or any UK-specific regulation are addressing the wrong qualification context. The frameworks are ILO-OSH 2001 and ISO 45001:2018 — and they must be referenced in connection with the specific scenario, not as standalone theoretical statements.

NEBOSH NGC: Unit Structure, Assessment, and UK Legislative Content

The NEBOSH NGC also consists of two units. NG1 — Management of Health and Safety — is assessed by an Open Book Exam with the same 48-hour format and 15-task structure as the IGC's IG1. NG2 — Risk Assessment — is assessed by the same practical workplace risk assessment report format as the IGC's IG2.

NG1 OBE answers must reference UK domestic legislation. The Health and Safety at Work etc. Act 1974 is the foundation: Section 2 establishes the employer's duty to ensure the health, safety, and welfare of employees so far as reasonably practicable; Section 3 extends the duty to non-employees affected by the employer's undertaking. The Management of Health and Safety at Work Regulations 1999 operationalise these duties — Regulation 3 requires suitable and sufficient risk assessment; Regulation 5 requires health and safety arrangements proportionate to the nature and scale of risks.

Beyond these core instruments, NG1 questions on specific hazard types require knowledge of the relevant specific regulations: COSHH 2002 for chemical and biological hazards; RIDDOR 2013 for injury and dangerous occurrence reporting requirements; Manual Handling Operations Regulations 1992 for musculoskeletal risk; PUWER 1998 for work equipment safety. Candidates who can name the regulation, identify its core duty, and connect it specifically to the scenario hazard will consistently outperform those who reference "relevant legislation" without naming it.

Grading thresholds for NG1 are identical to IG1: Distinction 80%+, Credit 65–79%, Pass 45–64%, Refer below 45%. The NG2 practical grading criteria are the same as IG2.

Which Should You Choose: A Decision Framework

The right qualification is determined by where the candidate works and which laws and frameworks govern their workplace — not by which qualification is perceived as "better" or "harder." Both qualifications are at RQF Level 3, assessed to the same standard, and lead to the same progression routes. Neither is superior to the other. Each is appropriate to a different context.

Choose the NEBOSH IGC if: you are working outside the UK — particularly in the Middle East, Asia, Africa, or multinational environments; your employer specifies NEBOSH IGC in the job description; you are working for a multinational organisation that requires a globally portable H&S qualification; you are based in the UK but plan to work internationally and want broader geographic recognition.

Choose the NEBOSH NGC if: you are based in the UK and regulated by UK health and safety law; your employer specifically requires knowledge of UK legislation including HSWA 1974, MHSWR 1999, COSHH, or RIDDOR; your role involves applying UK-specific regulations directly and your employer needs confirmation of that legislative competency.

One grey area arises for UK-based candidates working for international employers. In this case, confirming the employer's preference before enrolling is the practical solution — some UK employers of international candidates accept either qualification, while others specify one. Neither qualification is significantly harder than the other in terms of the study commitment required. The difference is entirely in the legislative content learned and applied in OBE answers.

Both qualifications lead to the same destination: IOSH professional membership, NEBOSH Diploma eligibility, and professional recognition in their respective markets. Employer Recognition: Where Each Qualification Is Accepted

NEBOSH IGC is the qualification of choice for employers in the Gulf region — UAE, Saudi Arabia, Qatar, Kuwait, Bahrain, and Oman — where multinational oil, gas, construction, and facilities management companies specify it as the baseline H&S credential. Across South and South-East Asia, including India, Malaysia, Singapore, and Hong Kong, IGC is similarly embedded as the standard professional H&S qualification. In sub-Saharan Africa — Nigeria, South Africa, Kenya, and Ghana in particular — IGC is recognised by multinationals operating in extractive industries, construction, and logistics.

NEBOSH NGC is the standard UK employer qualification for H&S officer and safety manager roles. UK employers in construction, manufacturing, facilities management, healthcare, and public sector organisations routinely specify NGC as the minimum H&S qualification for operational and management roles. Some UK employers accept either IGC or NGC; others specifically require NGC because they need to verify that the holder understands the UK legislative framework their organisation is legally required to follow.

The difference in employer recognition is not a quality difference — it is a geographic fit difference. An IGC holder applying for a UK role that specifies NGC will sometimes need to demonstrate their knowledge of UK legislation separately; an NGC holder applying for an international role may need to supplement their qualification with evidence of familiarity with ISO 45001 and ILO-OSH 2001. Neither scenario is insurmountable, but choosing the right qualification for the intended market from the outset avoids the issue entirely.

Progression From IGC or NGC: Identical Routes to Diploma and IOSH Membership

From the point of qualification, IGC and NGC lead to the same progression pathway. Both qualify the holder for IOSH TechIOSH or AssocIOSH membership — the entry-level professional designations within the IOSH membership structure. Both are accepted as the prerequisite academic qualification for NEBOSH Diploma enrolment.

At Diploma level, the legislative framework difference between IGC and NGC becomes less relevant — the Diploma requires candidates to engage with both international management frameworks and relevant legislation from multiple contexts, depending on the unit and the candidate's workplace setting. The IGC-to-Diploma and NGC-to-Diploma routes are treated equivalently by NEBOSH and by IOSH in assessing applications for GradIOSH membership and subsequently for the Chartered Member (CMIOSH) professional review.
