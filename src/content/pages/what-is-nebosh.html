What NEBOSH Is and What It Does

NEBOSH — the National Examination Board in Occupational Safety and Health — is an Ofqual-regulated awarding organisation founded in 1979 and headquartered in Coventry, UK. It designs and awards occupational health and safety qualifications to candidates across 130+ countries through a network of over 700 Approved Learning Partners (ALPs). NEBOSH is not a regulatory body and does not enforce health and safety law. That function belongs to national regulators such as the Health and Safety Executive (HSE) in the UK. NEBOSH awards the qualifications that train practitioners to understand and apply the principles those laws require.

This distinction matters in practice. When a candidate completes the NEBOSH IGC, they hold a qualification awarded by an Ofqual-regulated body. When they reference ISO 45001 in their IG1 answers, they are applying a framework that NEBOSH has determined reflects international H&S management standards. The qualification is the credential. The legislation and management systems studied within it are the content. Neither replaces the other, and neither is NEBOSH itself.

Approximately 90,000 to 100,000 assessment sittings take place under NEBOSH qualifications each year globally, across certificate and diploma levels. The qualification range spans entry-level awareness awards through to degree-equivalent diploma programmes — each level designed for a different career stage and a different depth of professional H&S responsibility.

Every NEBOSH Qualification Explained: Certificates, Diploma, and Award

NEBOSH awards qualifications at three tiers. The Award sits at the introductory level, designed for general workforce H&S awareness rather than professional practice. The Certificate qualifications — the most widely held NEBOSH credentials globally — provide a professional working knowledge of H&S management for those with safety responsibilities. The Diploma is an advanced qualification for dedicated safety professionals operating at a strategic or senior management level.

Within the Certificate tier, seven distinct qualifications cover different contexts and industries. The NEBOSH International General Certificate (IGC) is the most widely held NEBOSH qualification globally, designed for candidates working under international H&S frameworks and recognised by employers in the Middle East, Asia, Africa, and across multinational markets. The NEBOSH National General Certificate (NGC) covers the same H&S management principles with a UK domestic legislative focus, referencing HSWA 1974 and associated UK regulations, and is the standard entry qualification for UK-based H&S roles.

The NEBOSH Certificate in Fire Safety specialises in fire risk management, referencing the Regulatory Reform (Fire Safety) Order 2005. The NEBOSH Certificate in Environmental Management covers environmental legislation and ISO 14001, and is taken by those responsible for an organisation's environmental performance. The NEBOSH National Construction Certificate is designed for the construction industry, covering CDM Regulations 2015 and construction-specific hazards. The NEBOSH Certificate in Health, Safety and Wellbeing (HSW) is designed for non-safety professionals — HR managers, operations leads, and line managers — who need a foundational understanding of H&S without the depth of the IGC or NGC. The NEBOSH Certificate in Process Safety Management (PSM) is a specialist qualification for the oil, gas, and chemical industries.

At Diploma level, the NEBOSH International Diploma and NEBOSH National Diploma are assessed across three units (DN1, DN2, DN3) by extended academic written assignments. They sit at RQF Level 6 — degree-equivalent — and are the primary academic route to Chartered Membership of IOSH (CMIOSH), the professional standard for senior H&S practitioners.

NEBOSH Qualification Levels: What Each Level Means for Your Career

NEBOSH qualifications sit within the Regulated Qualifications Framework (RQF), which provides a standardised measure of qualification level and size in England and Wales. Understanding the RQF level of a NEBOSH qualification tells you both the academic standard expected and the career stage at which it is appropriate.

The NEBOSH Award sits at RQF Level 2 — equivalent to GCSE standard. It provides an introductory understanding of workplace health and wellbeing and is intended for the general workforce rather than for those with professional H&S responsibilities. It is not a professional qualification and does not satisfy employer requirements for a safety officer or management role.

The NEBOSH Certificate qualifications sit at RQF Level 3 — equivalent to A-Level standard. This is the entry point for professional H&S practice. Certificate holders are equipped to manage health and safety responsibilities within their workplace, apply risk assessment principles, understand relevant legislation, and fulfil H&S officer or safety-responsible manager roles. The certificate qualifications are assessed by a combination of Open Book Exam (OBE) and a practical risk assessment, and require approximately 100–130 guided learning hours.

The NEBOSH Diploma sits at RQF Level 6 — equivalent to undergraduate degree standard. It requires degree-level academic work: critical analysis of H&S management systems, synthesis of academic literature, strategic management recommendations, and Harvard referencing. Diploma holders are qualified for senior dedicated H&S practitioner roles, H&S management directorships, and independent consultancy. Study typically requires 400–500 guided learning hours across three units, spread over 12–18 months for working professionals.

How NEBOSH Assessments Work: Open Book Exams and Practical Assessments

NEBOSH replaced traditional invigilated written examinations with Open Book Exams (OBE) for certificate units in 2020. The OBE format gives candidates a 48-hour window to complete all assessment tasks using a provided workplace scenario document. Tasks are submitted via the NEBOSH online candidate portal. There are no examination centres and no invigilated conditions — the assessment is scenario-based and application-focused rather than knowledge-recall focused.

Six command words appear across the 15 tasks of a certificate OBE: Suggest, Describe, Explain, Outline, Justify, and Identify. Each command word signals a specific required response depth. Identify tasks require a brief named list. Outline tasks require the key points without extended detail. Describe tasks require a full detailed account. Explain tasks require causal reasoning — not just what but why. Suggest and Justify tasks require a reasoned proposal or recommendation connected to the scenario evidence.

The practical assessment — IG2 for IGC candidates, NG2 for NGC candidates — is completed separately from the OBE. Candidates conduct a workplace risk assessment in their own workplace, documenting hazards, risk ratings (likelihood × consequence), control measures following the hierarchy from elimination to PPE, and priority actions. The practical report is submitted via the NEBOSH portal independently of the OBE window and is graded on its own criteria.

Diploma units are assessed differently — not by OBE but by extended written academic assignments submitted to NEBOSH. Each of the three Diploma units (DN1, DN2, DN3) produces a major written assignment requiring academic referencing, critical analysis, and management-level argumentation.

Where NEBOSH Qualifications Are Recognised Globally

NEBOSH qualifications are recognised by employers in 130+ countries, with particularly high demand in markets where multinational employers operate alongside local regulatory frameworks that lack an equivalent national H&S qualification standard. In the Gulf region — UAE, Saudi Arabia, Qatar, Kuwait, Bahrain, and Oman — NEBOSH IGC is widely specified in H&S job descriptions by both multinational and large national employers. In South and South-East Asia — India, Malaysia, Singapore, and Hong Kong — NEBOSH holds similar standing as the benchmark entry-level H&S credential for professionally managed organisations.

Across sub-Saharan Africa — Nigeria, South Africa, Kenya, and Ghana in particular — NEBOSH is recognised as the international standard qualification for safety professionals in extractive industries, construction, and multinationals with African operations. In the UK, both IGC and NGC are recognised by employers, with NGC specifically preferred where knowledge of UK domestic legislation is required. In Ireland and Australia, NEBOSH qualifications are accepted alongside local equivalents.

The Ofqual regulation status of NEBOSH qualifications provides the quality benchmark that drives this international recognition. Employers in markets without a national qualifications framework equivalent to the UK's RQF use Ofqual regulation as a proxy for qualification credibility and rigour. Approximately 50,000 to 60,000 new NEBOSH certificate holders qualify each year globally.

NEBOSH and Professional Membership: The Route to IOSH Chartered Status

NEBOSH and IOSH (Institution of Occupational Safety and Health) are separate organisations that serve complementary functions. NEBOSH is the awarding body — it designs and awards qualifications. IOSH is the professional membership body — it confers membership grades on qualified practitioners and promotes the profession. Holding a NEBOSH qualification does not automatically grant IOSH membership, but NEBOSH qualifications are the most widely used academic route to each IOSH membership grade.

NEBOSH Certificate holders (IGC or NGC) can apply for IOSH TechIOSH (Technician Member) or AssocIOSH (Associate Member) status — the entry-level professional designations within the IOSH membership structure. These designations are recognised by employers as confirmation that the holder meets a baseline standard of professional H&S competency.

NEBOSH Diploma holders are the primary candidate group for IOSH GradIOSH (Graduate Member) status — the pre-Chartered professional grade that signals advanced academic and practical H&S competency. From GradIOSH, candidates can progress to CMIOSH (Chartered Member of IOSH) through a competency-based professional review, requiring a minimum of two years' practising H&S experience at an appropriate level. CMIOSH is the professional standard of excellence for H&S practitioners in the UK and is increasingly specified in senior H&S job descriptions globally.

For candidates studying any NEBOSH qualification, understanding this professional pathway from the outset shapes how they approach their qualification — the Diploma in particular is most valuable when treated as the academic foundation of a CMIOSH application, not simply as a standalone credential. NEBOSH Grading: Distinction, Credit, Pass, and Refer

NEBOSH certificate OBE units use a four-band grading system. Distinction requires a score of 80% or above. Credit requires 65–79%. Pass requires 45–64%. A score below 45% is a Refer — the NEBOSH term for a fail — which requires the candidate to resit the unit in a future OBE window using a different scenario document.

Practical assessment units (IG2, NG2) are graded on report quality criteria rather than percentage scores: the breadth and accuracy of hazard identification, the quality of risk matrix application, the appropriateness of control measures and their hierarchy, and the standard of priority action recommendations. The qualification is awarded only when both the OBE unit and the practical assessment unit have been passed.
